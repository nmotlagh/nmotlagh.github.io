This work studies retrieval-augmented selective QA. For each question, it
compares the model's direct answer with the answer produced after retrieving
evidence and revising, then measures whether the revision helped, hurt, or made
no difference.

The contribution is to treat that as two separate signals rather than one.
Answer confidence estimates whether the draft is already correct; a distinct
signal estimates whether revising with retrieved evidence will improve it. A
routing policy needs both — to decide when to answer directly, when to revise
with evidence, and when to abstain — rather than relying on confidence alone.
The evaluation spans NQ-Open, TriviaQA, and PopQA with DPR and BM25 retrieval
over a shared Wikipedia source.

The paper is in preparation for ARR submission; its title and author list are
withheld here to preserve anonymous review.
