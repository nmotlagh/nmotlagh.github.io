A retrieval-augmented QA system can improve an incorrect answer, but it can also replace a correct answer with a mistake. Average accuracy alone does not describe this tradeoff.

This dissertation research evaluates both branches for the same question: a direct answer and a revision using retrieved evidence. The paired outcomes distinguish answers that are **preserved**, **repaired**, **harmed**, or **unrecovered**. With binary correctness, the net change in accuracy is repair minus harm.

The central distinction is between **confidence** in an initial answer and **recoverability** under a particular revision process. The work studies how these signals inform a choice between answering, refining, and abstaining.

My contribution includes the evaluation pipeline, model training, and experiments comparing decision policies. This overview describes unpublished dissertation research; it is not a peer-reviewed publication or a released preprint.
