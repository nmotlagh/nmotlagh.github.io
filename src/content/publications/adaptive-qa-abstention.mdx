A retrieval-augmented QA system can improve an incorrect answer, but it can also replace a correct answer with a mistake. Average accuracy alone does not describe this tradeoff.

This dissertation research evaluates both branches for the same question: a direct answer and a revision using retrieved evidence. The paired outcomes distinguish answers that are **preserved**, **repaired**, **harmed**, or **unrecovered**. With binary correctness, the net change in accuracy is repair minus harm.

The central distinction is between **confidence** in an initial answer and **recoverability** under a particular revision process. The work studies how these signals inform a choice between answering, refining, and abstaining.

## Setup

- **Data:** 25,870 held-out questions from NQ-Open, TriviaQA, and PopQA.
- **Retrieval:** three setups over Wikipedia: DPR dense retrieval, BM25, and BM25 reranked with MonoT5.
- **Models:** Llama 3.1 8B Instruct writes the drafts and the revisions. Llama 3.3 70B Instruct is the automated semantic judge. gpt-oss-20b and OLMo 3 7B are secondary generator families.
- **Policies:** LoRA-trained scorers choose between answering and revising, and can also abstain in the three-action menu.

## Rigor

- Three training seeds, compared with paired run-level t-tests.
- 10,000-replicate bootstrap intervals for the baselines.
- Thresholds are selected on the dev split and frozen before test.

## Stack

PyTorch, Hugging Face Transformers, vLLM, FAISS, and GPU jobs on Slurm.

The manuscript was revised after ACL Rolling Review, and an arXiv version is in preparation. Code and artifacts will not be released.
