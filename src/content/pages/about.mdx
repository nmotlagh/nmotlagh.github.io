My research asks what an AI system should do when directly returning its current best answer is not yet justified. I study three points in the answering process: when a classifier should abstain from an unreliable prediction, whether visual evidence moves a model's scoring of an ambiguous input toward the intended interpretation, and whether refining a question-answering draft is more likely to repair it than harm it.

I successfully defended my PhD dissertation, *Answering Under Uncertainty: Abstention, Ambiguity, and Recoverability*, at The Ohio State University on July 8, 2026. I remain a PhD candidate while final revisions and committee sign-off are completed, with the degree expected in August 2026. I am advised by Jim Davis.

I am first author on four published papers and have a fifth manuscript under review at ACL Rolling Review. The published work covers natural reject-option classification (Springer Best Paper Award at ISVC 2022; MVA 2025 journal extension), evidence-use metrics for multimodal machine translation (WMT 2024), and a temporal satellite-imagery collection framework (ICCV Workshop 2021). The current manuscript studies paired direct and evidence-refined outcomes in retrieval-augmented QA; its submission title, author list, and preprint remain withheld during double-blind review.

I write training code in Python/PyTorch, build evaluation harnesses and calibration dashboards, and run distributed experiments on Slurm/Singularity clusters.
