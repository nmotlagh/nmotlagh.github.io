I’m a machine learning researcher and engineer with a PhD in Computer Science and Engineering from The Ohio State University. I build training pipelines and evaluation tools to study when models should answer, use more evidence, or abstain.

My dissertation, *Answering Under Uncertainty: Abstention, Ambiguity, and Recoverability*, connects three questions: when a classifier should withhold a prediction, whether a multimodal model uses its evidence, and whether revising an answer helps or hurts. My work includes four first-author peer-reviewed papers and the Springer Best Paper Award at ISVC 2022. My research on retrieval-augmented answer revision is listed separately as unpublished work.

I work as a Computer Engineer II at DCS Corp. Previously, I conducted research in Ohio State’s Computer Vision Lab and completed five summers of AFRL-sponsored research. My hands-on work includes Python and PyTorch, Hugging Face models, LoRA fine-tuning, and experiments on Slurm and Singularity clusters.

I earned my PhD in August 2026, advised by Prof. Jim Davis, with graduate minors in Mathematics and High-Performance Computing. I also hold an M.S. (2025) and a B.S. with Honors (2021) from Ohio State, and have reviewed for CVPR, ICCV, and ECCV.

I’m based in Columbus, Ohio and am interested in moving to California or New York City. I’m looking for research engineering, ML software engineering, and applied scientist roles where I can turn research questions into working systems and careful evaluations.
