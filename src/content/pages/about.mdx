My research asks what an AI system should do when directly returning its current best answer is not yet justified. I study three points in the answering process: when a classifier should abstain from an unreliable prediction, whether visual evidence moves a model's scoring of an ambiguous input toward the intended interpretation, and whether refining a question-answering draft is more likely to repair it than harm it.

I completed the requirements for a Ph.D. in Computer Science at The Ohio State University after successfully defending my dissertation, *Answering Under Uncertainty: Abstention, Ambiguity, and Recoverability*, on July 8, 2026. Formal degree conferral is scheduled for August 9, 2026. My advisor is Jim Davis.

I am first author on four published papers and have a fifth anonymous manuscript in preparation for ACL Rolling Review resubmission. The published work covers natural reject-option classification (Springer Best Paper Award at ISVC 2022; MVA 2025 journal extension), evidence-use metrics for multimodal machine translation (WMT 2024), and a temporal satellite-imagery collection framework (ICCV Workshop 2021). The current work studies whether retrieval-conditioned revision repairs or harms an observed short-form QA answer, and whether intervention-conditioned value can guide return-versus-revise decisions. Its evaluation is bounded to a fixed open-domain QA stack; the submission title, author list, numerical results, and preprint remain withheld during anonymous review.

I write training code in Python/PyTorch, build evaluation harnesses and calibration dashboards, and run distributed experiments on Slurm/Singularity clusters.

I am a Technical Analyst II at DCS Corp, where I work on applied machine learning research and evaluation. I am strongest on teams working on LLM evaluation, calibration, retrieval-augmented systems, selective prediction, or reliability infrastructure. Based in Columbus, OH. U.S. citizen with five summers of AFRL research experience.
